I am a legal researcher, lecturer, and dual-qualified practitioner with a background in cross-border commercial and corporate litigation.

I hold an Adjunct Lecturer position at the School of Law of the University of Nicosia, where my teaching has covered private international law, insolvency law and banking law. I am also an Associate Researcher at the University’s Procedural Law Unit.

I have written on topics ranging from civil procedure and the use of judicial analytics in the evaluation of judges, to jurisdiction and applicable law in AI-related cross-border disputes. My work has appeared in Cypriot and European commentaries, including the first scholarly commentary on the Cypriot Civil Procedure Rules, with further work forthcoming, inter alia, in the Journal of Private International Law and in the Edward Elgar EAPIL series.

In the course of my academic work, I have been called to advise the Cyprus Bar Association on the governance of legal information in Cyprus, and I regularly appear before parliamentary committees of the House of Representatives to speak on judicial and civil procedure reform.

My education and practice span three jurisdictions: England and Wales, where I read law and later cross-qualified as a Solicitor; the United States, where I completed my LLM; and Cyprus, where I have practised as an Advocate in commercial and corporate litigation. I draw on all three continually, bringing to my research a comparative appetite, a measure of pragmatism, and (usually) a pull towards questions on which academic and practitioner interests converge.

My current research projects sit across private international law, comparative civil procedure, legal analytics, and judicial systems and AI. Among these, I have interdisciplinary and empirical work in progress on the legitimacy, independence and accountability implications of large language model use in judicial decision-making, and on the decision-making behaviour and performance of such models, particularly on conflict of laws problems and in multilingual contexts.

I am applying to doctoral programmes for 2027 entry, and am always happy to hear from prospective supervisors or collaborators whose research interests intersect with mine.

If you see an alignment with my work, feel free to reach out at maou.y@unic.ac.cy.
