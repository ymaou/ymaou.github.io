Research

I am an Associate Researcher at the Procedural Law Unit of the University of Nicosia.

Over the past three years I have worked on private international law, comparative civil procedure and international commercial arbitration, mainly with doctrinal and comparative methods. More recently my research has turned to judicial decision-making and AI, and to the performance of LLMs in adjudication.

Areas of research

Private international lawprimary

I am a private international law scholar by training. I studied Conflict of Laws under Professor Linda J. Silberman at New York University School of Law, and thereafter, I spent five years of practice in cross-border commercial litigation, advising and representing clients on matters of jurisdiction, applicable law, and the recognition and enforcement of judgments, mainly under the European Regulations. I have also been teaching private international law at the University of Nicosia since 2024.

My current research interests centre on the interaction between the EU conflicts framework and AI-caused liability claims; the structural gaps in the Cypriot framework and the case for codification; and the conflicts-related performance and blind spots of LLM tools used for legal advice and judicial decision-making.

Ongoing projects

  • Recognition and enforcement of AI-assisted judgments (11th Journal of Private International Law Conference, Zurich 2027)

    I am working on a paper titled ‘To Trust or Not to Trust? AI-Assisted Judgments and the Future of Mutual Trust under Brussels Ia’, co-authored with Dr Nicolas Kyriakides. This work asks what becomes of mutual trust when AI-assisted judgments circulate across the Union free of exequatur. The paper has been accepted for presentation at the 2027 Journal of Private International Law Conference in Zurich.

    This is the second paper in our line of work on the interaction between the existing European instruments and AI. Our first paper, ‘Does It Still Fit? Assessing the Suitability of the EU’s Private International Law Framework in the Wake of AI-Related Non-Contractual Cross-Border Disputes’, is forthcoming in the Journal of Private International Law.

  • European Association of Private International Law (EAPIL) Young Research Network, 5th Research Project (publication forthcoming)

    This is an ongoing project of EAPIL’s Young Research Network, running over eighteen months. The study examined the rules on applicable law in privacy and personality rights across twenty-three jurisdictions, with the aim of informing the revision of the Rome II Regulation. As one of two National Rapporteurs for Cyprus, I co-wrote the Cyprus report and discussed the comparative findings at the project conference held at the University of Luxembourg in March 2026. Our work is forthcoming as a chapter in the project volume with Edward Elgar.

  • Cypriot Private International Law (treatise, Hippasus Publishing, expected 2027)

    I am part of a team of five authors writing the first scholarly treatment of Cypriot private international law, in Greek. The work covers the European Regulations, the residual Cypriot common law, and arbitration.

  • Choice of law for digital products (working paper, presented at the 2nd Riga PIL Conference)

    I have ongoing research on the application of the product liability rule in Article 5 of Rome II to digital products. I presented this work at the 2nd Riga Private International Law Conference in June 2026. My paper, titled ‘The Achilles Heel of Rome II: Article 5, “Marketing”, and Digital Products’, is currently in progress.

Judicial decision-making and artificial intelligenceprimary

I came to this field through my prior work in judicial analytics. In 2023 I conducted a self-directed study of judicial analytics as a potential tool for the evaluation of judges in Cyprus, which I presented at the Procedural Law Unit’s 3rd Annual Symposium and published in the symposium volume. The study introduced judicial analytics to Cypriot legal scholarship, proposed a traffic-light framework for the reliability of judicial-performance data, and assessed the data-infrastructure, language and regulatory preconditions for an analytics-informed system of judicial evaluation in Cyprus.

My current research focuses on the use of LLMs in judicial decision-making. I study the institutional conditions under which AI-assisted adjudication can be reconciled with judicial independence, accountability and procedural fairness, as well as human oversight models for the judge–LLM interaction. I am also engaged in interdisciplinary research on the performance of LLMs on legal reasoning tasks, particularly in multilingual and multi-jurisdictional settings and on problems of private international law.

Civil procedure and comparative procedural lawsecondary

I came to the subject of comparative civil procedure through my background as a litigator in Cyprus, and later cross-qualification as a solicitor of England and Wales, which required close study of the English CPR.

In 2023, Cyprus replaced its colonial-era civil procedure rules with a direct translation of the English CPR. My research focuses on this reform, and specifically on the conditions for the success of the Cypriot CPR 2023 as a legal transplant; the overriding objective and the cultural conditions for its operation in a pre-Woolf procedural culture; and the authority of English CPR doctrine in the Cypriot post-reform era.

Ongoing projects

  • Cypriot Civil Procedure (N Kyriakides and others eds, Nomiki Bibliothiki 2025)

    I have written on interim applications and remedies in the first scholarly commentary on the new Cypriot Civil Procedure Rules. This is a collaborative work bringing together more than twenty Cypriot academics and practitioners. My contribution is in chapter 14, where I presented the position under the CPR 2023, integrating comparative analysis of English practice and case law. The commentary is being revised as case law under the new Rules develops.

  • Empirical study of Cypriot procedural reform (self-directed, in progress)

    I am currently conducting an empirical study of interim applications decided since 2023, recording the Cypriot courts’ treatment of English authority and the extent of their adherence to the overriding objective. The project is currently at data analysis stage (dataset available on request).

International commercial arbitrationsecondary

I studied international arbitration at New York University School of Law, where my Masters specialisation was in International Business Regulation, Litigation and Arbitration, under the programme direction of Professor Franco Ferrari. I wrote my thesis under his supervision, on the enforceability of unilateral arbitration clauses. Arbitration has also been part of my practice as a litigator in Cyprus, where I have acted in proceedings for the recognition and enforcement of foreign arbitral awards and for interim measures in aid of arbitration.

Given my Cypriot background, my research interests have centred on the treatment of international instruments such as the New York Convention and the UNCITRAL Model Law in small, post-colonial jurisdictions. I am particularly interested in recognition and enforcement trends and in how enforcement patterns compare across jurisdictions studied on a common taxonomy.

Ongoing projects

  • Empirical study of arbitral award enforcement in Cyprus (self-directed, in progress)

    I am conducting an empirical study of recognition and enforcement applications in Cyprus under the New York Convention, using the complete publicly available population of first-instance decisions from 2006 to 2026. The decisions are hand-coded based on the Alford and others taxonomy. Data collection and analysis are complete, and my working paper is currently in progress (dataset available on request).

Legal informatics and free access to lawsecondary

I came to this field as a practitioner, through the ordinary difficulty of finding Cypriot case law. Cyprus is the only EU Member State in which court judgments are not published by a state actor. Access depends almost entirely on CyLaw, a private non-profit platform run by the Cyprus Legal Information Institute under the auspices of the Cyprus Bar Association, on terms that restrict re-use and automated processing.

In this context, my research focuses on the consequences of data scarcity for the development and performance of LLMs; on frameworks for access to legal information and the state’s role in providing it; and on publication bias and its effect on the development of doctrine.

Ongoing projects

  • Cyprus’s legal information gap (Cyprus Bar Association, ongoing)

    My self-directed research on the governance and publication of Cypriot court judgments led the Cyprus Bar Association’s Committee on Civil Procedure Rules and Reform to invite me to present it and to advise on reform. I continue to advise the Committee on legal information policy, and to develop this line of research.
